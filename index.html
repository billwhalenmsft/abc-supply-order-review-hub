WHY THIS MATTERS

Make prior orders usable at the moment a seller needs them

A repeat-order or repair question should not require a seller to search across history, decode old line descriptions, guess whether two colors are equivalent, and manually rebuild the order context.

Find the right prior order

Retrieve one exact order or repair context instead of widening the search and mixing unrelated history.

Reconstruct the order accurately

Keep the recorded product description, material, color, quantity, and unit together so details are not lost in translation.

Clarify product and color

Compare recorded order language with a curated reference and label the result as a match, a candidate, or something a person must resolve.

Prepare the next action

Produce a review-ready packet with the evidence, open questions, and human decisions needed before a quote or reorder.

Value thesis: give sellers a consistent way to answer “What did they buy, what color/material was recorded, and what must we confirm now?” without turning historical data into an automatic commercial decision.

THE PROPOSED EXPERIENCE

Five steps from question to review-ready order context

The workflow is designed for outside sales, inside sales, and order-support teams handling repeat-order, repair, and historical-product questions.

  1. 1. Ask in plain language. “Show the prior order for this job,” “What color was recorded?” or “Prepare the repair-order review.”
  2. 2. Retrieve one exact history set. Bring back the intended order header and line details without blending nearby orders.
  3. 3. Reconstruct every relevant line. Preserve product description, material, recorded color, quantity, unit, and source order reference.
  4. 4. Run product and color review. Compare the recorded description with a curated product-family and palette reference; show evidence and uncertainty.
  5. 5. Produce an unsubmitted review packet. Summarize what is known, what may match, and what a person must confirm before the next action.

FIRST ORDER-MANAGEMENT MODULE

Product, material, and color matching—with uncertainty made visible

The goal is not to make a visual guess. It is to connect the exact language recorded on an earlier order to a governed reference and show how strong—or weak—the match actually is.

Recorded order truth

  • Manufacturer or product-family language recorded on the line.
  • Material and color name as captured in order history.
  • Original quantity and unit, unchanged.

Curated comparison

  • Look for the same named product, material, and color.
  • Show candidate references when the language is close but not conclusive.
  • Keep product-family and palette evidence attached to the result.

Match status

  • Recorded match: the same description is present in both sources.
  • Candidate: a person must validate a possible product or color relationship.
  • Cannot determine: the available evidence is insufficient.

Current demonstration reference

The prepared proof uses a curated reference of 18 product families and 66 named palette entries. They are unpriced, quote-required references—not verified sellable SKUs, current availability, or approved physical-color equivalence.

Important: the experience does not infer an exact color from a photo, silently convert materials, or treat a possible match as an approved substitution.

WHAT THE SELLER RECEIVES

A review packet built for action—not another search result

The output brings the order facts and the unresolved decisions together so a seller or order-support specialist can continue without redoing the investigation.

Order and line context

  • Exact prior-order reference and relevant line items.
  • Product, material, recorded color, quantity, and unit.
  • Source notes that explain where each fact came from.

Matching evidence

  • Recorded matches and candidate references.
  • Explicit cannot-determine results.
  • No hidden substitution or unit conversion.

Questions for a person

  • Which product or color relationship needs validation?
  • Is the recorded item still available and appropriate?
  • Which current commercial terms must be sourced separately?

Clear next action

  • Ready for human review—not ready for automatic submission.
  • Open questions assigned before quote or reorder activity.
  • A repeatable handoff instead of an informal summary.

THE REVIEW ASK

Decide whether this is the right first order-management pilot

The immediate decision is not whether to automate order submission. It is whether the read-only review experience solves a recurring seller problem well enough to validate with a bounded cohort.

1. Confirm the problem

Do repeat-order and repair questions create enough search, reconstruction, color ambiguity, or rework to justify a pilot?

2. Choose the first scenario

Recommended: one repair or repeat-order scenario that requires exact prior-order retrieval plus product, material, and recorded-color review.

3. Name the owners

Identify the business owner, order-source/API owner, identity/security owner, support owner, and approved pilot cohort.

4. Agree on the scorecard

Set baselines and targets for time-to-answer, first-pass resolution, clarification/rework, and human acceptance.

Recommended next move: validate the repeat-order plus product/color review scenario with the account team, then define the smallest read-only pilot that can measure the four outcomes above.
